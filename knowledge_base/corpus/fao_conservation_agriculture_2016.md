# Save and Grow in Practice: Sustainable Cereal Cropping Systems

## Breaking Continuous Monoculture Cereal Systems
Continuous cereal monocultures deplete specific soil micronutrients and foster virulent soil-borne pathogens and grass weed complexes. Incorporating a non-host broadleaf break crop (e.g., canola, brassicas, or pulses) in a multi-year rotational cycle breaks pathogen life cycles, restores soil physical friability, and can increase subsequent grain yields without requiring additional synthetic nitrogen fertilizers.

## Conservation Agriculture: Minimum Disturbance and Soil Biota
Mechanical moldboard plowing shears earthworm burrows and pulverizes soil aggregates into vulnerable dust particles susceptible to water and wind erosion. Transitioning to zero-tillage or direct drilling preserves continuous vertical macro-pores created by decaying root channels and earthworms, tripling water infiltration rates and reducing surface crusting.

## Managing Excessive Precipitation and Hydrological Flow
In regions subject to episodic high-intensity rainfall or poor surface drainage, field-scale conservation measures such as contour vegetative bioswales, grassy waterways, and constructed infiltration swales mitigate ponding and waterlogging. These hydrological interventions intercept excess runoff, prevent surface soil anoxia, and filter agricultural sediment before discharge into natural water bodies.
