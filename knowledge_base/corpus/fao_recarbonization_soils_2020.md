# Recarbonizing Global Soils: Management Practices for Cropland & Semi-Arid Systems

## Soil Organic Carbon Dynamics in Water-Limited Croplands
In arid and semi-arid agroecosystems where soil organic carbon (SOC) is depleted, microbial activity, macro-aggregate stability, and infiltration capacity become severely restricted. Topsoils with depleted carbon exhibit reduced cation exchange capacity and low biological water-holding resilience. Monoculture cereal cultivation without legume integration or biomass return steadily depletes the labile carbon pool.

## Legume-Based Intercropping and Cover Cropping
Introducing drought-tolerant legume species (such as cowpea *Vigna unguiculata*, pigeon pea *Cajanus cajan*, or chickpea *Cicer arietinum*) into cereal rotations produces dual agronomic benefits: biological nitrogen fixation through rhizobial symbiosis and continuous root carbon deposition. Under appropriate conservation management, legume cover cropping and residue retention in semi-arid wheat systems can contribute to soil organic carbon, enhance mycorrhizal colonization of primary crop roots, and provide nectar forage for local wild pollinators.

## Surface Residue Retention and Moisture Conservation
Maintaining soil surface residue cover can reduce evaporation losses compared to clean-tilled bare soil. Surface residues buffer soil temperatures against extreme diurnal spikes and preserve fungal hyphal networks necessary for macro-aggregate water stability.
