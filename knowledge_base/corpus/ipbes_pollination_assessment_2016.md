# IPBES Pollination Assessment: Agricultural Drivers and Mitigation Strategies

## Landscape Homogeneity and Pollinator Decline
The expansion of intensive monoculture cropping and the widespread removal of non-crop linear field elements (hedgerows, grass margins, ditch banks) have dramatically diminished floral diversity and nesting resources for wild pollinators. In landscapes with less than 10% non-crop semi-natural habitat, pollinator richness and insect flower-visitation frequencies experience steep non-linear declines.

## Native Flowering Hedgerows and Floral Strips
Re-establishing multi-species perennial hedgerows composed of indigenous flowering shrubs and perennial forbs provides season-long pollen and nectar forage. Native flowering margins can support wild bee species richness while simultaneously harboring predatory ground beetles (Carabidae) and hoverflies (Syrphidae) that suppress common agricultural crop pests.

## Pesticide Risk Mitigation and Integrated Pest Management
Replacing prophylactic broad-spectrum insecticide and herbicide sprays with Integrated Pest and Nutrient Management (IPNM) prevents acute and sub-lethal disorientation of beneficial wild insects. Establishing vegetative buffer strips between sprayed crop zones and natural habitats significantly reduces pesticide droplet drift and aquatic ecotoxicity.
