# IPCC Special Report on Climate Change and Land: Dryland Degradation & Agroforestry

## Climate-Soil Interactions in Semi-Arid Drylands
In dryland agricultural regions characterized by low precipitation and high thermal regimes, land degradation manifests through accelerated soil carbon oxidation and loss of vegetative ground cover. When monoculture cropping is practiced on bare exposed soil, high surface temperatures can drive extreme atmospheric vapor pressure deficits and high evaporative moisture loss.

## Agroforestry and Microclimate Dampening
The integration of deep-rooted woody perennials and nitrogen-fixing trees into cropping systems (alley cropping, parkland agroforestry) modifies the boundary layer microclimate. Tree canopies can reduce wind exposure, moderate daytime surface temperatures through shading and transpirational cooling, and improve soil moisture retention across the crop root zone.

## Land Restoration and Habitat Corridors
Halting deforestation and planting multi-strata agroforestry corridors across fragmented agricultural landscapes enhances ecological connectivity. Reconnecting native vegetation patches mitigates desertification frontiers, prevents wind-induced topsoil deflation, and establishes ecological refugia for biodiversity under intensifying climate extremes.
