# Agroforestry Enhances Soil Biodiversity and Microbial Biomass: Meta-Analysis

## Soil Biology under Agroforestry Systems
Continuous monoculture cropping limits the structural heterogeneity of organic substrates entering the soil system, causing a marked depletion of soil microbial biomass carbon and fungal diversity. The cited local synthesis attributes evidence from comparative field studies to introducing woody perennials alongside annual crops, with reported benefits for soil microbial biomass and arbuscular mycorrhizal fungal (AMF) communities across arable topsoils. The original meta-analysis and its quantitative results require external verification.

## Below-Ground Root Niche Partitioning
The coexistence of shallow lateral crop roots and deep vertical tree root architectures prevents competitive exclusion and maximizes nutrient uptake efficiency. Deep tree roots intercept leaching nitrate ions from the subsoil, recycling nutrients to the surface via seasonal leaf litterfall and root turnover, thereby recarbonizing the profile and supporting higher populations of subterranean earthworms (*Lumbricidae*).
